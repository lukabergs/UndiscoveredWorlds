# Multi-scale consistency plan

Written 2026-10-10 by the lead session (Fable 5.1) from the user's brief and the research conversation of 2026-10-09 and 2026-10-10. It is the plan for making every zoomed-in map of a world (the regional maps, at any scale down to one metre per pixel) consistent with the global map it comes from, exactly, and for the features the user wants to recognise at those scales: visible erosion, Earth reference windows beside every map, folded and curved mountain ranges. It stands beside `erosion-hydrology-plan.md` (whose Phases E, R, R2 and E5 built the regional pass this plan restructures) and follows `world-model.md`, `consolidation-plan.md` and `docs/reference-guides/agent-workflow.md`. Section 2 records the research so no agent re-runs it. Decisions marked **user** need the user's answer before the step that depends on them; everything else is decided here and undone by a commit.

This plan is run end to end by one orchestrating session that dispatches subagents (§7). The user's only action is to point a session at §7 "Next". That is a deliberate exception, asked by the user on 2026-10-10, to the rule that a session's handoff prompt is printed in the chat and never saved in a plan: this plan carries its own handoff in §7, and only this plan does.

## 1. Decisions taken on 2026-10-10

- **The problem.** The regional pass lowers the mean elevation of the land it refines (window B of erosion run 9: the land's median block mean 198 m below the global map's, largest 3.2 km; `erosion-0052`), because channels are incised and the solve lowers the land between them while nothing rises. The world's own erosion run already lowered each global cell by its denudation, so the regional pass counts the denudation twice. The global and the regional map disagree about the same place. The user wants zoom to any resolution, down to 1 px = 1 m, with no such disagreement.
- **The contract.** Every field the global grid owns is authoritative at the global scale. For an intensive field (elevation, temperature, precipitation) the fine field's mean over a global cell equals the global value; for an extensive field (discharge, sediment) the fine total equals the global total; for a subgrid statistic the global grid carries (relief amplitude, channel share, later drainage density) the fine field reproduces it. The fine pass is free below that: valleys below the mean, interfluves above it, never a shifted mean. Any effect that only appears at fine scale and matters to the coarse one is put into the global model as a law or a field, never fed back by running fine everywhere (§2, "The scale question").
- **The mechanism: a hierarchy of refinements with an exact projection at every level.** The regional pass becomes a loop over levels. Level 0 is the global grid; level L has cells `factor^L` times finer (`refinement_factor`, 4 by default, 2 allowed; decided by the S2.6 measurement). Each level interpolates its parent, adds the detail that belongs to its cell size, then removes the detail's mean over every parent cell exactly, distributing the removal over the parent's children with weights that keep channel beds, sea and lake beds where they are and lift the land between them. Exactness is by construction, to float rounding, at every level, so every level agrees with every coarser one and with the global grid; the projection reads one parent cell, so the pass stays local, lazy and bit-identical on overlapping windows. The present smoothing correction (`mean_passes`, approximate: 9.4 m after six passes) remains as a pre-step that keeps the per-parent multipliers slowly varying; the projection after it makes the result exact.
- **Why not the alternatives** (§2): a globally constrained correction is exact and smooth but couples every block in the window, so it cannot be seamless; constraining the global block only and leaving intermediate levels soft loses the agreement between levels (a k = 4 map would not be the k = 16 map averaged); redefining the global value as the ridge or pre-incision surface (Schott et al. 2024's retargeting) builds the climate on a biased height; keeping the drop and shallowing the incision hides the inconsistency.
- **Detail belongs to the level whose cell size matches it.** Noise octaves, tributaries, incision, hillslopes, valley floors, glacial troughs and cirques, fold fabric each have a level. This is also the answer to "the terrain looks too smooth": on erosion run 9 a global cell is about 150 km and a k = 16 fine cell about 10 km, and the ridge-and-valley texture of eroded landscapes lives at valley spacings of 1 to 5 km. The hierarchy puts it where it exists instead of asking a 10 km cell to show it.
- **Output at any resolution.** Exactness holds on the level grids (integer `factor^L` cells per global cell). A requested metres-per-pixel value is served from the next finer level by box averaging, which preserves means; an aligned grid is exact, an unaligned one is a resample and the report says so. Levels finer than about k = 256 (600 m on a 256-column world) are render tiles computed on demand, never stored as bundles: a 10 km window at 1 m is 100 M cells per field.
- **CPU is the reference, the GPU comes last.** Every level's rules are per-cell kernels with no dependency between cells except through the parent and the pinned channels, so a GPU port is a port and not a redesign. The reference implementation stays on the CPU, where the tests and the overlap checks run. The port (Phase S4) uses OpenGL 4.3 compute shaders, already in the toolchain through SFML and vendor-neutral; the user's RTX 3080 runs them. CUDA and soillib are not used (§2). GPU results need not be bit-identical to the CPU's; they must be bit-identical to themselves across windows.
- **Earth reference windows** are part of every review page from Phase S1 on: named windows of ETOPO 2022 (and finer open DEMs later) rendered at the same metres per pixel and the same palette as the map beside them, with the features to look for named. They are comparison inputs, never forcing (AGENTS.md).
- **Folding is two features, not one.** Plateaus and curved ranges (Tibet and its eastward flow, the Bolivian orocline) come from the lithosphere as a thin viscous sheet under an indenter, a tectonics-engine change (Phase S6, its design by a Fable agent at S6.0). Fold trains (the Zagros, the Subandean belt) are a regional fabric at the 5 to 20 km level fed by the engine's cumulative shortening and its direction (Phase S5, which needs the engine to export them: a change of the tectonic output contract, **user**).
- **The erosion plan's Phase F** (render-detail gully filter) is subsumed: gullies are a level detail of this hierarchy, not a renderer filter.
- **Modules and ledgers.** The hierarchy and the Earth windows live where the regional pass lives: `src/simulations/landscape/` (`uw_landscape`), `src/cli/regional_command.cpp`, `configs/parameters/landscape.json`, `scripts/evaluation/`, `configs/evaluation/regional.json`; runs are `regional` runs; ledger entries go to `runs/ledger/erosion.jsonl` as Phases E to E5 did. The engine steps (S5.1, S6) use the `tectonics` ledger; a new world field (S3.6) uses the `world` ledger. Seed 1 everywhere; erosion run 9's bundle and the judging windows A, B, C of regional runs 13 to 15 are the fixtures (A `--pixels 164,48,183,63`, B `--pixels 180,48,199,63`, C `--pixels 152,40,215,71`).

## 2. Research recorded (2026-10-09 to 2026-10-10)

### The scale question

Dynamic simulations, unlike procedural detail functions, move mass by their own rules, so a fine simulation drifts from the coarse map it started from. Letting the fine result define the coarse map would require computing fine everywhere (the planet at k = 16 is 8 M cells and about 130 windows of 20 s per pass, before any time integration), which is infeasible. The way out, used by every climate and landscape model, is closure: the coarse model carries laws for the statistical effect of fine processes (the stream power law is itself such a closure of metre-scale hydraulics), calibrated once offline or against Earth data, and the fine pass realises what the closure already accounted for. The three regimes and the terms: top-down with a constraint (this plan), bottom-up everywhere (infeasible), closure by parameterisation (the heterogeneous multiscale method, subgrid orography in weather models). Closure fails only when one specific fine configuration, not a statistic, changes the coarse outcome (a river capture moving an outlet, a lake that exists only at fine resolution): those become coarse-grid objects or are accepted as the coarse world's.

### The exact projection, worked out

Let a parent cell `c` at level L−1 have value `v_c` and children `C(c)` at level L (`factor²` cells). After interpolation and the level's detail the children hold candidates `F*`. With weights `w(x) ≥ 0` (0 on channel beds, sea cells and lake beds, 1 elsewhere; if all weights in `C(c)` are 0, all become 1), set `F = F* + λ_c · w(x) / mean_{C(c)}(w)` with `λ_c = v_c − mean_{C(c)}(F*)`. Then `mean_{C(c)}(F) = v_c` to rounding (double accumulation). The multipliers `λ_c` differ between neighbouring parents, so a step of `|Δλ| · w` appears along parent borders. Its size (corrected at the S0.4 review, 2026-10-10; the first version of this paragraph said `I / 12` and `I / 60`, which do not follow from the operator): a channel line one level-L cell wide crossing a parent of `factor²` children with incision `I` lowers the parent's mean by `I / factor`, so `λ = I / factor`, and with the channel cells at weight 0 the other cells have `mean(w) = (factor − 1) / factor` and rise by `I / (factor − 1)`: `I` at factor 2 and `I / 3` at factor 4 (300 m and 100 m for `I` = 300 m). A single incised cell gives `I / (factor² − 1)`. The step a cell sees across a parent border is the difference of the two parents' lifts `λ_c w / mean_c(w)`, which is what the projection reports (`maxborderstep_m`, `borderstep99_m`), with `|Δλ|` beside it. A smooth deficit field barely helps for a line (a river) because the residual of a line after smoothing is most of the line, so the choice is the factor, measured in S2.6: factor 4 is recommended for the smaller step, factor 2 for the finer grading of levels. Every global land cell has a pinned channel (`pin_area_km2` 0), so neighbouring parents carry comparable deficits and `Δλ` is small except where valley floors differ in width. Derived fields (temperature by the lapse rate, climate class) follow the elevation and are reported, not projected; discharge on the pinned channel at a global cell's outlet equals the global discharge by construction and S2.3 tests it (`refinement_levels_tests.cpp`, §3 "Tests"; the first version said S2.2). Lake surfaces are per global cell and are inherited, not averaged.

## 3. Design

### Fields and units

- Levels: `L = 0` is the global grid (`W x W/2`, cell-centred). Level L cell `(i, j)` has parent `(i div factor, j div factor)` and absolute 64-bit key `j * width_L + i` (at 1 m on a 256-column world `width_L` is about 67 M and the key exceeds 32 bits). Every rule is a pure function of the absolute level-L position, the seed and the parent's values within the level's halo (`agent-workflow.md`, "Learned in Phase E").
- The regional bundle keeps its format (`src/io/regional_bundle.{hpp,cpp}`; manifest blocks `window` and `source`) and gains `level`, `factor` and, for a resampled output, `metres_per_pixel` and `resampled: true`. World bundles stay identical in every step except S3.6, which adds fields.
- Parameters: group `landscape.refinement` in `configs/parameters/landscape.json` (`factor` 4, `projection_weight_channel` 0, `stored_scale_max` 256 (the finest k written as a bundle), `tile_cells` 1024, `detail_cells` 2 (a detail runs at the coarsest level whose cells are at most its size over this), plus each level detail's own keys named in its step), read as `tuning::landscape::refinement::*`; `scripts/parameters.py` regenerates `landscape_parameters.hpp`.

### Kernels (`src/simulations/landscape/`, namespace `landscape`, planet-free)

- `refinement_grid.{hpp,cpp}`: level grids, parent and child index arithmetic, window snapping outwards to whole parent cells, halo in parent cells per level and its conversion to the next level, the absolute key. Generalises `regional_grid` (which stays for the k-based callers until S2.3 retires them).
- `refinement_projection.{hpp,cpp}`: the exact projection of §2 with weights; a function that reports the largest parent-mean error of a level (must be 0 to rounding); the box-average resample to a requested metres per pixel with its alignment report.
- `refinement_levels.{hpp,cpp}`: the loop over levels: prolongation (`upsampling`'s Catmull-Rom generalised to one factor step), the level's detail hooks in a fixed order (noise octave, channels and tributaries, incision, solve, valley floors, glacial troughs and cirques, fold fabric, coast and lake rules), the pre-smoothing, the projection, the level's diagnostics. `regional_pass.cpp` becomes the caller of this loop.

- The existing level details keep their files (`regional_surface`, `regional_valleys`, `regional_troughs`, `regional_cirques`, `regional_ice`, `regional_coast`, `regional_edges`, `regional_water`) and gain a level argument; each is assigned a level by its characteristic size in kilometres (R9's lesson: sizes on the ground, converted per level).

### S2.3's projection weights and detail levels (design, 2026-10-10)

Second answer of the `design` agent (Fable 5.1, read-only), after S2.3's first version showed holes to −7.5 km and border steps to 11 km on erosion run 9 (§6, S2.3). Unverified by the agent: that B's and C's 7.9 km λ are the lake hole's shadow; the feasibility argument assumes a global land cell's value is above the sea level. The orchestrator's addition: the single pass for a K that is not a power of the factor keeps today's behaviour bit for bit (the band test and the uncapped lake depth act inside the loop only), so it stays a reference.

1. **Bounds, not weights.** Per child: a rate (0 on land channel beds, `projection_weight_channel`; 1 otherwise) and headrooms up / down in metres: land ∞ / F* − max(s+, L+) (L the highest surface of its wet 8-neighbours or own lake cell, `regionalwater` step 5); sea s− − F* / ∞; wet (lake surface > 0, channel or not) S− − F* / ∞. The same shift δ on every rated child, capped at its headroom in λ's direction; δ by iteration inside the parent (row-major absolute order, double, ≤ f² rounds: δ = remainder / uncapped count, children with less take theirs and leave). When the caps exhaust, the remainder goes to the beds uniformly (`bedsmovedparents`), else to every child ignoring caps (`forcedparents`, `maxforcedmove_m`): exactness always, flips only in counted parents. A land-only parent of a global land cell is always feasible (Σ headroom = f²(mean F* − s+) ≥ f²|λ|); a failure means beds above the mean or a fine mask against the global class. Depth weights and `wetallpinnedparents` go; `driedcells`, `shoresbelowlake` stay as checks; `landbelowsea` added. ### S2.7: smooth before exact (design, 2026-10-10)

1. **Cause.** A cell's step is `|δ_c − δ_c'|`, `δ_c = λ_c f² / Σ r` over the children with room (≈ 16 λ / n_land), `λ_c = v_c − mean_c(F*)`. ## 6. Questions answered in advance, risks, open items

- **The border step.** The exact projection prints a step of `|Δλ| · w` along parent borders (§2). A channel line crossing a parent lifts its land by `I / (factor − 1)` (a single incised cell by `I / (factor² − 1)`; §2, corrected at the S0.4 review), and the step is the difference of neighbouring parents' lifts; it is measured in S2.6 by its statistics (largest, 99th percentile) and by the look. If factor 4 still prints, the fallback is a projection over the grandparent (exact to the grandparent, approximate between adjacent levels), recorded as a decision then.
- **All-pinned parents.** A parent whose children are all channel, sea or lake beds has no land to lift; the weights fall back to uniform and the beds move. Expected only in global sea cells and wide valley floors; the count is a diagnostic.
- **Halo growth.** Each level needs a halo in parent cells for its details (the solve about two cells, the smoothing two per pass, the troughs one). A window at level L needs a parent window of its size over the factor plus the halo, recursively; the total extra work is geometric and small, but the top levels of a tiny deep window still read tens of global cells. S2.1 computes and reports the halo chain.
- **Memory and size.** Stored bundles stop at `stored_scale_max` (k ≤ 256 on a 256-column world); finer levels are tiles (S4.1). The 5 MiB blob rule applies to anything tracked; runs are local-only.
- **The lapse-rate clamp.** Temperature follows the elevation linearly except above the sea level clamp, so its block mean is not exactly preserved; reported, not enforced. If the user wants it enforced, it becomes a projected field with the same operator.
- **Exactness and rounding.** Means are accumulated in double; the test bound is 1e-3 m. "Zero error" in the user's sense means this bound, stated in every entry.
- **Resolution dependence of stream power on D8.** The incision per level comes from the global rates (R9), not from a fine steady state, so the depth does not depend on the level; the channel share and the valley spacing do, and S2.6's `resolution-invariance` check reports them so S3.2 can correct what drifts.
- **What the minimal world cannot show.** No mountains, lakes or ice at 128 x 64; every behaviour step also runs on windows of erosion run 9's bundle, as the rows say.
- **S3.6 changes world bundles.** New diagnoser fields only; the step check shows every other field identical and the access is measured because writes change.
- **S5.1 is a public contract change** (tectonic output contract): **user**. S6 changes the engine's deformation: the user's verdict on its judging run is required before it is promoted into the baseline recipe.
- **GPU determinism.** OpenGL compute results are deterministic per driver for per-cell kernels without atomics; S4.3 states the tolerance to the CPU and proves the GPU's own overlap identity. If the driver breaks either, `--gpu` stays off by default.
- **Fable's usage limit** during reviews: the `reviewer` agent runs with `model: opus` and the entry says so (agent-workflow).
- **Open for the user** (asked in §7 as they arise): the factor (S2.6), the verdict on S3.6's windows, S5.1's contract change, the promotion of S6.
- **Learned in S0, S1, ...**: appended by the orchestrator after each step from the subagents' "Insights for the plan" sections.

- **S3.0 survey (surveyor, the valley-spacing law and network statistics).** Perron, Kirchner and Dietrich 2009 (Nature 460, 502–505; Methods read from the PDF at geodynamicsprogram.whoi.edu): ∂z/∂t = D∇²z − K A^m |∇z| + U (n = 1); Pe = K L^(2m+1) / D; setting Pe = 1 gives the characteristic length Lc = (D/K)^(1/(2m+1)); first-order valley spacing 6.4 Lc ≤ λ ≤ 12.7 Lc (longer slopes, wider spacing); λ independent of the erosion rate; wider spacing from larger D, smaller K or smaller m. Field sites (Table 1, D/K in m^(2m+1), m, Lc, λ): Dragon's Back 12, 0.42, 4.0, 30 m; Point of the Mountain 26, 0.31, 7.5, 54 m; Napa Valley 86, 0.35, 13.7, 128 m; Gabilan Mesa 124, 0.35, 17.2, 163 m; Eaton Hollow 802, 0.37, 46, 321 m (λ/Lc 7.0 to 9.5). So first-order spacing on Earth is 30 to 320 m: the law applies at the finest levels only; coarser levels need a Hack/Horton hierarchy. ## 7. How this plan is run

### The orchestrating session

The user starts a session with `--model opus` (Opus 5.5, effort high) and gives it one line: `Run docs/architecture/multiscale-plan.md: read §7 and continue from "Next".` The same line restarts the work after any interruption; nothing else is needed from the user. The session is the orchestrator of `agent-workflow.md`: it holds the step list, briefs subagents, integrates, checks, writes ledger entries and commits, and never takes a design decision the plan does not settle.

Working loop:

1. Read §7 "Next", then only what it points to: the step rows, the §1 decisions and §3 parts they name, §5, and the ledger entries named. Do not read the whole plan or the erosion plan.
2. Build the order: steps sharing a Group letter run at once in worktree workers from the same commit (`isolation: worktree`; the brief names the commit; a worktree has no `refs/`; results come back as `git diff` applied to the main tree); other steps run alone in the main tree. Launch independent agents in one message and keep working while they run.
3. For each step: brief (below) → the agent works → its summary comes back → the orchestrator runs the step's named checks on the merged tree (the step check and the tests; never more) → `reviewer` for a behaviour step, with the plan step, the diff and the draft entry and nothing of the author's reasoning → fixes by the same worker through SendMessage → ledger entry (`runs/ledger/<module>.jsonl`, the fields of the existing entries: id, date, module, parent, phase, duration, hypothesis, change, expected, observed, not_run, lessons) → one commit (Conventional Commits, `feat(regional): ...`, `test(landscape): ...`, `docs(multiscale): ...`; the attribution line of the session's system reminder).
4. After every commit: write the step's Duration and status into §4, paste the agent's "Insights for the plan" into §6 under "Learned in S<phase>", and rewrite "Next" below. This happens after every step, not only at phase closes, so an interruption loses nothing.
5. At a phase close: the rows marked `orch`, the judging runs, the status note under the phase table, the memory note (`architecture-and-workflow-direction`), and "Next" pointing at the first step of the next phase. Phases S1 and S2 run in parallel after S0.5 (disjoint files); S5.1 waits for the user's approval and the orchestrator runs everything else meanwhile.
6. Context budget: keep agents' summaries, never their transcripts; read with searches and excerpts; when the session's context grows long, write "Next" and stop with one line in the chat saying so; the user restarts with the same pointer.

When to involve the user, and only then: a **user** decision that gates every remaining step; a deletion under `refs/` or `runs/`; a public contract change (S5.1); a visual verdict that a phase close requests. Write the question into "Next" under "Questions for the user" with the recommendation and continue with what does not depend on it. Never block waiting in the chat.

When a step raises a design question the plan does not settle: record it in §6, continue with independent work; if the step cannot proceed, launch a `design` agent (`general-purpose`, `model: fable`, effort high) with the question, the plan section and the files, read-only; paste its answer (≤ 500 words) into §6 or §3 and proceed. Effort escalation (`xhigh`, `max`) only after a failed attempt at the agent's effort, recorded in `lessons`.

### The brief (what the orchestrator gives a subagent)

A subagent's brief is its only context. It states: the goal in one sentence; this document and the step row; the §1 decisions and §3 contract the step implements (quoted, not pointed to, when short); the files to change and the files to leave alone; what is ruled out and why (§2, §6); the exact check commands and their expected results; what not to run (no full ctest, no Python suite, no runs beyond those named, no GUI); the commit to start from and whether to commit (workers in worktrees do not commit; the orchestrator does); the rules of §5 that apply (Python scripts only, Write tool for multi-line edits, `uv` from PowerShell, absolute paths into the main tree for `refs/`, no links into a worktree); and the report format below. A brief that cannot be written clearly means the step is not ready: the orchestrator refines the row first.

### The report (what a subagent hands back)

At most 400 words, in this order: **Done** (what changed, files); **Checks** (each command, its result, the numbers observed); **Not run** (and why); **Insights for the plan** (facts the next steps need, worded for pasting into §6: what surprised, what the tests missed, what a later step must know); **Open questions** (for the orchestrator, marked **user** when they are the user's). No reasoning, no transcript. The orchestrator pastes "Insights for the plan" into §6 verbatim under the phase's "Learned in" heading, attributing the step.
