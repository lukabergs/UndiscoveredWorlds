# Noise functions: findings and the choice for the regional pass

Written 2026-10-11 (the multi-scale plan's orchestrator, Fable 5.1) from a surveyor's web survey and the code. The user's question: the coastlines look blobby and low in detail, a straight coast runs along 58 E at 7 to 9 N, and a grid-like bias was suspected in the Perlin noise; which modern alternative (Simplex, OpenSimplex, SuperSimplex, others) fits this project best.

## 1. What the project uses today

- `src/simulations/landscape/spherical_noise.cpp`: improved Perlin noise (Perlin 2002: the quintic fade, the 12 cube-edge gradients chosen by a 64-bit hash `% 12`, no permutation table), sampled in 3D at each cell's unit vector on the sphere, so there is no seam and no pole stretch. `FractalNoise` sums octaves of wavelength `wavelength_km / 2^k` with weight `persistence^k`; an octave is dropped when its wavelength falls outside `[shortest, longest)`, which is how each refinement level draws only the band between its parent's spacing and twice its own. Each octave gets a hashed translation and **no rotation**: every octave's cubic lattice shares one orientation, with one axis on the pole axis. Users: the global relief roughness, the regional band noise per level, the coast decision noise, the domain warp, the lake-shore and ice-edge noise.
- `channel_network.cpp` `legpath`: one-dimensional cosine-interpolated value noise along river legs (hashed nodes).
- `src/core/procedural_noise.cpp`: the legacy diamond-square fractal and its warps, used by the older terrain and climate stages (about 25 call sites).
- `src/simulations/geology/plate_tectonics/simplexnoise.cpp`: a 4D simplex noise on a torus mapping, private to the tectonics engine.

## 2. The candidates (survey of 2026-10-11)

| Noise | Lattice | Directional artefacts | Band | Cost vs Perlin | Licence / source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perlin 1985 | cubic | axis clumping, second-order discontinuities on lattice planes | weakly band-pass | 1 | public |
| Improved Perlin 2002 | cubic | clumping removed by the 12 edge gradients; the lattice stays cubic; Lagae et al. 2010 still measure "an undesired axis-aligned anisotropy" and horizontal features from the permutation hash (not ours: we hash) | weakly band-pass | 1 | public |
| Simplex (Perlin 2001) | simplex (skewed) | "no noticeable directional artifacts" (Gustavson 2005) | weakly band-pass | lower complexity in nD (O(n²) against O(n 2ⁿ)) | patent US 6,867,776 expired 2022-01-08 |
| OpenSimplex (2014) | stretched honeycomb, larger kernel | smoother, fewer artefacts | weakly band-pass | slower than simplex | public domain |
| OpenSimplex2 (F) / OpenSimplex2S (the renamed SuperSimplex), KdotJPG | 3D: a rotated body-centred cubic lattice (the union of two rotated cubic lattices) | the README's purpose; 2S "has better uniformity in 3D and 4D"; `ImproveXY/XZ` variants for a plane sampled in 3D (not our case: we sample a sphere) | weakly band-pass | F about as fast as simplex; 2S a little slower (no published benchmark) | CC0 / Unlicense, github.com/KdotJPG/OpenSimplex2 |
| FastNoiseLite | OpenSimplex2, 2S, cellular, Perlin, value; fBm, ridged, ping-pong, domain warp | as its kernels | | | MIT, single header |
| Better gradient noise (Kensler et al. 2008) | cubic with a decorrelated hash and a projection for surfaces | improves axial decorrelation | better band-limited | slower | paper only |
| Wavelet noise (Cook and DeRose 2005) | a stored tile of coefficients | an axis-aligned square in the spectrum from the separable B-spline | almost perfectly band-limited | slower; needs the tile | paper, Pixar code |
| Gabor noise (Lagae et al. 2009) | sparse convolution of oriented kernels | none; anisotropic on purpose when wanted | band-limited, controllable | costly (high splat density) | paper |
| Phasor noise (Tricard et al. 2019) | Gabor kernels' phase field | none; makes coherent oscillating patterns (stripes, ridges) with an orientation field | band-limited | as Gabor | paper |
| Worley / cellular | hashed feature points | cell structure is the point | not band-limited | 1 to 2 | public |
| Value noise | cubic, interpolated hashed values | lattice visible at low octaves | weakly band-pass | cheapest | public |

Facts that bear on the choice (Lagae et al. 2010, "A survey of procedural noise functions"): dropping octaves ("frequency clamping", which our per-level band rule does) "works best if the noise is narrowly band-pass... Perlin noise is only weakly band-pass"; simplex noise is no better in this respect (no source found for OpenSimplex2's spectrum). Sampling a 3D noise on a surface "will not result in a band-limited texture, even if the 3D function is perfectly band-limited" (the normal direction's frequencies fold in); integrating along the normal (their projection) keeps the band. No published measurement of lattice alignment on spheres was found.

## 3. What the maps actually show, and what is not the noise's

Measured on regional run 42 (window A at k 64) and runs 33 and 36 (the same window at k 4 and 16), 2026-10-11:

- **The straight coast at 58 E, 7 to 9.5 N** is a 63-cell vertical run at fine column 336 at k 64, a 22-cell run at column 84 at k 16 and a 6-cell run at column 21 at k 4: the same line at every level, born at level 1 one fine cell inside global column 169, where the bilinear interpolation of the global elevation (sea cell 168 at −65 m, land cell 169 at +14 m, the same pair one row down) crosses the sea level along a straight vertical contour. The coast decision pushes the sea side down by up to `coast_sea_drop_m` (300 m) within the noise band and lifts the land side inland, so the 50 m coast noise cannot move the shoreline off the interpolated contour; the finer levels inherit the parent mask and their noise acts in the same locked band. The existing straight-run breaker (`edges.straightmax`) did not break it. This is a rule of the coast decision, not a property of the noise function.
- **The blobby coast** has the same cause: the shoreline is the smooth bilinear zero contour of the global elevation, decorated by noise in a thin band. The box-counting dimension measured in erosion plan R2 was 1.02 to 1.04 on windows A and B; Earth coasts measure 1.13 (Australia) to 1.25 (Britain) and 1.5 (Norway). More octaves do not help: the coast noise already reaches down to two fine cells, and at persistence 0.6 the sixth octave carries 5 % of the amplitude, about 2 m. What changes the dimension is noise that displaces the shoreline's position (a fractal domain warp down to the fine cells) and a decision whose gradient the noise can cross; erosion plan R2's warp (0.5 global cells at a 2-cell wavelength, no finer octaves) moved the dimension by 0.005 because a smooth displacement of a smooth line is a smooth line.
- **The SW–NE and NW–SE diagonal peaks** measured in S3.7 were the channel lattice's and the solve's (S3.10, S3.12 removed most of them); the residual N–S peak on window A is consistent with the locked coast above.
- Perlin's own cubic-lattice anisotropy is real but second-order here; its one weakness in our use is that every octave shares one lattice orientation (section 1), so whatever alignment exists stacks across octaves and is fixed to the pole axis.

## 4. The choice

1. **Kernel: OpenSimplex2S in 3D**, ported into `src/simulations/landscape/` (CC0; the reference implementation at github.com/KdotJPG/OpenSimplex2, 3D "fallback" orientation, no `ImproveXY` variant since the sphere has no preferred plane), selected by a new key `landscape.noise.kernel` (`"perlin"` keeps today's output bit for bit for parity runs; `"opensimplex2s"` the new default once judged). Reasons: no axis-aligned artefacts by construction (a rotated body-centred cubic lattice), the smoother of the two OpenSimplex2 variants (we want terrain, not speed), 3D available, cost close to Perlin's, public domain, well documented. Simplex 2001 is equivalent in artefacts but a patent-free reference implementation of the same quality is OpenSimplex2 itself; OpenSimplex 2014 is slower with no advantage over 2S.
2. **Per-octave rotation** for both kernels: each octave's sampling position is rotated by a hashed orthonormal matrix (from the octave's seed), so no lattice orientation is shared across octaves or tied to the pole axis. A pure function of position and seed: seamless, and the per-level band rule is untouched.
3. **Not chosen for the general noise:** Gabor and phasor noise (costly, and their strength is oriented patterns: the fold fabric of S5.2 is exactly such a pattern, and its design considers phasor noise for the fold trains with the orientation from `geosphere.shortening_direction`); wavelet noise (a stored tile, an axis feature in its spectrum, slower); Worley (feature-specific, not a terrain base); value noise (worse than what we have). FastNoiseLite as a dependency is not needed: one kernel is a few hundred lines.
4. **The band rule stays** (octaves dropped per level). Neither kernel is narrowly band-pass, so a dropped octave leaves some energy at the next level's wavelengths and a kept one some above the Nyquist; this is a known, small blur and aliasing, measured by the level-consistency check, and a projection along the normal (Lagae) would be the remedy if it ever shows.
5. **The coast is fixed in the decision rule, not in the kernel** (multi-scale plan S3.15): the straight contour lock, the enclosed sea pockets inside global land blocks, and the dimension target 1.2 to 1.3 on window A by a fractal position warp of the shoreline down to the fine cells.

Judging: the slope-spectrum anisotropy check of S3.7 (the lattice peaks per sector and level) and the coast's box-counting dimension on window A at k 64, the new kernel against the Perlin one at the same parameters; the user's look at the terrain and relief maps decides.
