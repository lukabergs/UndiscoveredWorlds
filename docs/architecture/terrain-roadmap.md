## 2. Rules for this roadmap

- One phase per session (memory "Phase sessions"); every step records its duration (ledger field, the Duration column below) and gets a ledger entry: pipeline steps in `runs/ledger/erosion.jsonl` (tectonics engine steps in `tectonics.jsonl`), sandbox steps as one entry per sandbox phase in `erosion.jsonl` with `"module": "erosion"` (the ledger tool accepts only the file's module), `"phase": "terrain sandbox ..."` and the commit range.
- Python runs from Git Bash as from PowerShell: `uv.exe` is on Git Bash's `PATH` (the `~/bin/uv.cmd` wrapper only forwards to it), so `uv run python scripts/sandbox/...` works in either shell (checked 2026-10-13). PowerShell is needed only for the `.cmd` wrappers (`msvc-env.cmd`, `cmake-vs.cmd`, `ninja-vs.cmd`) of a C++ build. In Git Bash, write scripts with the Write tool rather than heredocs (apostrophes and backslashes break them), never `cat > file` without input (it waits on stdin), and never name a scratch script after a standard-library module (`inspect.py` broke numpy's import).
- Sandbox steps are judged by the user's look (pages under `out/scratch/sandbox/<label>`, Earth atlas beside); no parity runs, no tests unless a bug persists. Pipeline steps keep the repository's checks (`step_check.py` at the minimal configuration for parity; one judging run where the change acts).
- The reviewer and any `design` agent run on Opus 5.5 (Fable 5.1 set aside by the user on 2026-10-17 for its token usage). Reviews follow each behaviour step of the pipeline (T2, T3, T4); sandbox steps are reviewed by the user's look.
- Storage (2026-10-17): `out/scratch` and `out/evaluation` are junctions to `D:/dev/undiscovered-worlds/out/` (so every page and run under them is on D); `out/build` stays on C. The session-start hook prints `scripts/uwtools/storage.py status`; agent worktrees are retired only through `scripts/uwtools/worktrees.py` (AGENTS.md).
- **user** marks a decision only the user takes; the orchestrator continues with independent work meanwhile.

### T4.12 design: continental growth as a world parameter (2026-10-16; a design agent on Opus 5.5, Fable off)

**Measurements** (read-only; run 78's checkpoints, unweighted cells of the engine's planar 256 x 128 torus; CRUST1.0 from `refs/source/physical/crust1-2013`, 1-degree cells weighted by cos(latitude), thickness from the bottom of the ice to the Moho, sediment included):
- The survey's "mean continental thickness 22.7 to 21.3 km" is the surface mean of `continental_crust` (continental volume per unit of the whole surface). Per unit of continental-class area the thickness is 34.7 km at the start and 26.2 km at 590 Myr.
- The 81 % continental class is mostly artefact: 14.6 % of the surface is "ghost" area (class 3 with under 1 km of continent, 6.6 km of basalt, at -5.8 km; 1.5 % at 110 Myr, 9.4 % at 310 Myr), 9.6 % hyper-thin (1-15 km). Crust thicker than 25 km covers 61.1 % at the start and 50.8 % at the end (mean 35.7 to 38.0 km); over 40 km grows 8.3 to 12.6 % (collisions). Likely ghost sources: areas are labels kept apart from volumes (`reconcileTransport` spreads a coarse cell's area over its 16 subcells, `tectonic_columns.cpp:227`; the overlap removes oceanic area first, `:578`; erosion and root flow move volume, never area, `:743`, `spreadRoots`).

**What the surveys found (step 1, read-only; file:line in the surveyors' reports, the main ones here).**
- **Only the engine is a torus.** Everything downstream is already a lat-lon sphere: `SphereMetric` (`src/simulations/landscape/sphere_metric.cpp`: exact band areas, dx = R dλ cos φ, longitude wraps, a neighbour beyond a pole is the cell itself) serves routing, rivers, stream power, lakes, the sandbox exe and the regional patches; climate has its own cell-centred `SphericalGrid` with exact areas and conservative remaps (`core/climate_grid.cpp`); validation weights by sin-band areas (`scripts/uwtools/grid.py`); ETOPO is resampled with cos weights (`prepare_earth_maps.py`). The torus enters the world at one place: `world_tectonics.cpp` copies the engine's cells 1:1 onto the lon-lat rows through `WorldGrid` (bilinear heights, nearest codes, wrap x, clamp y; `tectonic_world_grid.hpp:10-11`: "a sampling boundary, not a spherical tectonic simulation").
- **The engine** (13,630 lines; tests 5,933): both axes wrap (`geometry.cpp:120-126`, `lithosphere.cpp:74-99`); one square cell, `cell_m = world_width_km 1000 / W` with `world_width_km` 40,000 (`tectonic_scenario.hpp:76`), so the torus is 40,000 x 20,000 km = 800 M km^2, **1.57 times the sphere**, and its rows above 60 degrees, a third of its cells, stand for 13 % of the sphere's area. Plates are rigid 2-DOF translations in cells per update, angular velocity always 0 (`lithosphere.cpp:2460-2468`: "Version 1 solves planar translations, not torques or spherical rotations"; > 0.95 cell per update throws). Transport is a conservative subcell remap with bilinear 2 x 2 splats, wrapped in both axes (`tectonic_transport.cpp`, `tectonic_columns.cpp:250-264`); gaps fill with basalt and overlaps subduct from area coverage (`tectonic_columns.cpp:755-800`). Every budget weights cells equally (`cell_m^2`: plate area, masses, continental area, erosion, subduction, the growth reservoir; land shares as unweighted quantiles; plate seeds uniform per cell). The mantle flow is sin/cos with period 1 in x and y (`tectonic_dynamics.cpp:41-51`); the force balance is a dense 2P x 2P solve with contact normals on the grid axes. Initial relief and material are 4D simplex noise on a Clifford torus (`lithosphere.cpp:138-167`); process variation, ocean relief and the noise pass use integer wavenumbers in both periods. Stencils: 4-neighbour (plate growth, boundary BFS in cells, topology, provenance, margins), east/south face pairs (contacts, motion history, columns, noise), 8-neighbour explicit rivers, and implicit rivers on `landscape::torusmetric` (`tectonic_columns.cpp:486`, the only caller). Distances are wrapped Euclidean x `cell_m` (boundary graph, junctions, plumes). Fifteen test groups encode the seams (`BothPeriodicSeams...`, `WrappingOnY`, `ResamplingWrapsLongitudeAndClampsPolarRows`, ...).
- **Cost today:** 400 Myr at 256 x 128 in 477 s (the W3 identity run); 600 Myr 746-783 s at 256 and 168.5 s at 128 (factor 4.6 per doubling); no engine timing at 512. Cost per update is linear in cells (transport, contacts, BFS), N log N (Priority-Flood rivers), P N (ownership).
- **Other striping sources (the v17 diagnosis):** the sandbox's noise in planar equator-scale km (`terrain_sandbox.py` `Domain.mesh_km`, :209-211) and the pipeline's diamond-square fractal (`core/procedural_noise.cpp`, columns wrap, rows bounded; about 25 callers, among them `terrain.texturing` and `terrain.roughness`, which build the base world). `spherical_noise.cpp` (3D noise at the cell's unit vector) exists and serves the newer stages.
- **Format and windows:** `uw-run-bundle/v1` stores `grid {columns, rows, cell-centred, row-major north first}` with no projection key (`run_bundle.cpp:155-156`); a `grid` field is (rows, columns) for every consumer. Windows (`--pixels`, sandbox boxes, `regionalwindow`, the refinement hierarchy's lat-lon quadtree and its 64-bit keys) are all world-raster boxes. The `.uww` is a fixed 2-D stream. The desktop draws equirectangular images, one pixel per cell, no globe.
- **Measured baseline (seed 1, base-v9, sandbox global `t22-base-v9`, `out/scratch/sphere/step0/striping-t22-base-v9.json`):** east-west over north-south land gradient by band 0-30/30-50/50-60/60-70/70-80 degrees 0.90/1.20/1.52/2.12/3.08 (ETOPO 2048 1.04/1.04/1.30/1.26/1.07); aspect A4 final 0.030/0.035/0.091/0.169/0.285, procedural input 0.003/0.010/0.066/0.136/0.305 (ETOPO 0.014/0.015/0.024/0.062/0.037). The striping is in the input, as in v20.
- **Cell geometry (computed, `grid_numbers.py` in the session scratchpad):** the lat-lon grid at 256 columns has 156 km cells at the equator and 1.92 km east-west at the polar row (81 times narrower; 163 at 512); an equiangular cubed sphere with 64 cells per face edge has 24,576 cells (0.75 of the lat-lon count), edges 111-156 km, largest over smallest area 1.40 (N 128: 98,304 cells, 55-78 km).
- **Literature (surveyor, sources in its report):** Cortial et al. 2019 (Procedural Tectonic Planets) move plates by rotation about an axis through the centre (v = ω w x p) on a Fibonacci sampling with a spherical Delaunay triangulation, resample globally every 10 to 60 steps and handle collisions by boundary-triangle intersection (60k points: 0.19 s per step); goSPL keeps a fixed spherical mesh and interpolates displaced points back by inverse distance; GPlates composes finite rotations about Euler poles. No published system found runs climate on the mesh; goSPL and eSCAPE run landscape on it and take climate as input. Lat-lon with moving fields needs polar filters, merged polar cells or reduced grids (ECMWF's octahedral grid) against the converging meridians.

**Options.**
- **(a) Spherical engine on the lat-lon grid.** Plates rotate about Euler poles; budgets in true areas; the engine's grid is the world's, so the hand-off is an identity. Removes the north-south wrap and the pixel-isotropic fabric. But every engine stencil meets the converging meridians: cells 81 times narrower at the polar row at 256 columns, so the 0.95-cell displacement limit, cell-count distances (boundary BFS, arc distance `ceil(km/cell)`), 4-neighbour plate growth and any explicit diffusion in the column model (creep, collapse; explicit or not is unverified) become latitude-dependent, and 1.57 times the cells it needs at the equator's resolution; it needs polar filtering or merged polar cells besides the rotation work. Effort: the same rewrite of motion, transport and stencils as (b), plus the pole work; the result is worst exactly where the striping is.
- **(b) A spherical mesh for the engine, resampled to the lat-lon rasters (recommended).** The engine runs on near-uniform cells; one sampler at the hand-off publishes its fields on the world's W x W/2 grid, as `WorldGrid` does now. Removes the north-south wrap, the 1.57 area excess, the polar stretch of the tectonic fabric (a feature at 70 degrees has its true east-west size in km, so it appears stretched on the equirectangular map exactly as Earth's do) and every unweighted budget. Leaves the pipeline's own polar D8 bias above about 70 degrees (`erosion-hydrology-plan.md:273`) and the sandbox's and pipeline's planar noise (step T8.8 fixes the noise; the D8 bias is item 3's and (c)'s). Bundle format, `.uww`, windows, regional code, climate, sandbox, evaluation and the desktop are unchanged: the engine's public header and scenario keys change (asked below).
  - **Mesh choice: equiangular cubed sphere** (six faces, N x N cells each, cell id `face N^2 + j N + i`). Every cell has exactly four edge neighbours and four diagonal ones (seven at the eight cube corners), so the engine's 4- and 8-neighbour stencils and the landscape kernels' eight direction codes carry over through a neighbour table; point location is closed form (the face by the largest coordinate, then two arctangents), so the transport's forward splat stays O(1) per subcell; each face is a raster with a natural quadtree, the same shape as the refinement hierarchy, which keeps (c) open later. Against an icosahedral or Fibonacci mesh: those are more isotropic (no face axes) but need variable-degree stencils everywhere, a triangulation (no library in the tree; a dependency needs the user) and new transport; the cube's risk is an imprint along face axes and seams at face edges and corners, measured in acceptance. HEALPix's C++ package is GPL (only `healpix_bare` is BSD) and its rhombic cells suit equal-area statistics more than stencils.
- **(c) A spherical mesh through the whole pipeline.** (b) plus about 50,000 lines: `planet`'s `[x][y]` storage and its 150+ pixel wrap sites, terrain's pixel stamps and planar noise (9,600 lines), hydrology (2,650), society (1,660), the landscape kernels behind a table metric (13,100; moderate), the refinement hierarchy redone per face, the sandbox's raster Python (2,400), and a mesh-to-lat-lon export for climate (or a remap, 17,000 lines kept), the legacy regional map (24,400), the desktop and the imports. It changes the bundle format (a new grid layout and a cell table) and re-expresses every window: the public contract and save format, the user's decision. Gains over (b): no polar D8 bias, no third of the cells spent on 13 % of the area. Weeks to months; not now. The cubed sphere of (b) is the mesh (c) would adopt, face by face.

**Results that change.** Every procedural world and the tectonics recipe (new plate shapes, motions, areas; the W3 calibration was made on unweighted torus cells and must be re-measured with true areas, and may need one re-tuning round); the sandbox base (base-v10) and therefore its windows (re-chosen by `choose_windows.py`, same box format). Unchanged by design: the Earth and imported recipes (identity checked by `step_check.py`), Earth comparisons (they read the lat-lon rasters), the bundle and `.uww` formats, palettes and renderers.
